# The judgment pass, the tells no pattern catches

<!-- antislop: off: this page quotes the patterns it teaches -->

Read at stop 3 of deslop. The script catches words and fixed shapes; these need a reader. They come from the writing rules we use at gaja and from [humanizer](https://github.com/blader/humanizer) by blader, which builds on Wikipedia's [Signs of AI writing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Signs_of_AI_writing).

Strongest first. The first six justify a rewrite on one sighting; from seven on, act when two or more appear in the same passage.

## 1. The contrast across two sentences

> This does not mean the plan failed. It means the plan was early.

The first sentence denies something nobody claimed, so the second sounds larger. Say the second: *The plan was early.* Keep a contrast only when the reader holds the belief you correct.

## 2. The closing line that names what the paragraph showed

> That is the real lesson. / The message was clear. / And that changes everything.

Cut it. If the paragraph has a consequence it does not state, write the consequence as a fact.

## 3. The aphorism

> Growth is not a destination. It is a discipline.

Test: could a reader disagree from experience? If not, it is decoration: replace it with the specific claim, or cut it.

## 4. The echo

> They did not need more leads. They needed better systems.

The second sentence only amplifies the first. Keep the one that carries information, with its detail.

## 5. The invented specific

A number, a name, a date or a quote that the draft did not have and the user did not give. Never add one to replace a vague claim. Cut the claim or write `[add: ...]`.

## 6. Chat residue

"Great question", "I hope this helps", "Certainly!", "As an AI", a closing summary of what the text just said, an offer of more help. Cut it.

## 7. Three by default

Three examples, three adjectives, three short facts and a lesson. Keep the items the meaning needs; one strong example beats three thin ones.

## 8. The defence against nobody

An objection, or an option, raised only to be rejected: "you might think X", "a tempting approach would be Y". Cut it, unless a reader would really weigh it.

## 9. Epic tone on a small point

> And in that moment, everything about their pipeline changed.

Scale the sentence down to what happened: *The next week, two deals moved to proposal.*

## 10. Borrowed authority

"Experts agree", "studies show", a list of outlets that covered someone. Name the source and what it said, or cut the claim.

## 11. The clause that explains the clause before it

> We cut the price, which shows how much we care about customers.

Delete the explanation. The action is the evidence.

## 12. Decoration

Bold on every list item, an emoji on every heading, headings in Title Case, a horizontal rule between every section. Keep bold for the one thing a skimming reader must not miss.

## 13. Stacked hedges

"Could potentially", "might arguably", "it may be possible that". One hedge, where the doubt is real.

## 14. The same opening, sentence after sentence

Three sentences in a row that start with the same word. Merge two, or start one with the action.
